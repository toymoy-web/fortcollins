Common Shower & Bathtub Plumbing Issues

Worn shower valve cartridges are the most common issue we see, causing either a persistent drip or temperature that swings unexpectedly when another fixture in the house runs. Bathtub replacements, meanwhile, are more often driven by a homeowner's renovation plans than an active failure, though older cast-iron tubs occasionally develop leaks at the drain connection worth addressing before a full remodel. It's common enough to also need Faucet & Sink Repair that we always ask about it during diagnosis.

Diagnosis

How We Handle a Shower or Tub Job

For a valve issue, a technician identifies whether the cartridge, the pressure-balancing valve, or the supply lines themselves are the actual cause before replacing parts. For a full installation, we confirm rough-in measurements and drain compatibility with the new fixture before removing anything, so there are no surprises mid-project. Confirming those measurements ahead of time also means the old fixture isn't removed until we're certain the new one will actually fit and connect properly.

Extending a Valve's Working Life

Extending a Valve's Working Life

A shower valve's cartridge wears based on usage and water hardness, so a high-traffic bathroom on hard water will typically need cartridge service sooner than a guest bathroom that's rarely used. If you notice the handle getting stiffer to turn or the temperature becoming less precise over a period of months, that's usually the early stage of the same wear that eventually causes a full leak, worth addressing on your schedule rather than as an emergency. A fair number of these calls end up touching Plumbing Repair too, so we look at both together.

FAQs

Common Questions

Can you fix a shower that won't hold a consistent temperature?

Yes, this is usually a worn cartridge or pressure-balancing valve, a common and fixable issue.

Do you coordinate with a general contractor for remodels?

Yes, we regularly work alongside contractors on the plumbing portion of a bathroom remodel.

Can you replace just the tub without a full remodel?

Yes, a standalone tub replacement is possible as long as the existing rough-in is compatible or can be adjusted.

How long does a shower valve replacement take?

Most valve replacements are completed in a couple of hours, though tile or surround access can add time depending on the bathroom's layout.
