Diagnosis

How Mitigation Is Verified as Complete

Moisture meters check drywall, subfloor, and framing throughout the drying process, not just at the start, since materials can look dry on the surface while still holding damaging moisture underneath. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings return to a safe baseline, confirmed with equipment rather than a visual check alone. This same moisture data gets documented throughout the job, which is useful both for your own records and for any insurance claim tied to the event.

Reducing How Often You Need This Call

Reducing How Often You Need This Call

The single biggest factor in how bad a water event turns into is how quickly mitigation starts, which is largely out of your control once water is already in the house, but a few things help before that point. Keeping gutters and downspouts clear ahead of Fort Collins' June through August storm season reduces the chance of water finding its way toward your foundation in the first place. A tested, battery-backed sump pump is the other major factor, since most severe basement mitigation calls we get involve a pump that either wasn't there or wasn't working when the storm hit. Homes dealing with this often need Sump Pump Installation & Repair addressed around the same time.

FAQs

Common Questions

How is mitigation different from restoration?

Mitigation is the emergency drying and damage-limiting phase. Restoration is the rebuilding that follows once everything is confirmed dry.

Can you respond during an active storm?

Yes, our emergency line runs 24/7, including during active weather events.

How do you confirm everything is actually dry?

Moisture meters check materials throughout the process, not just visually, and drying continues until readings return to a safe baseline.

How long does mitigation typically take?

Extraction happens immediately, but full drying with equipment in place typically runs three to five days depending on how much moisture was absorbed.
