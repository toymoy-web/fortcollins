Local Cause.

Why This Matters More in Certain Fort Collins Neighborhoods.

Alta Vista and Buckingham remain inside the city's official 100-year floodplain today, per the city's own 2005 Northside Neighborhoods Plan, a direct legacy of how these working-class neighborhoods were historically built low near the Cache la Poudre River while wealthier development was pushed to higher ground. A working sump pump with a reliable backup power source is a genuinely higher-stakes investment for a home in one of these areas than it would be for a home built on higher ground. This overlaps with Water Damage Restoration often enough that we check for it on the same visit.

Signs to Watch For.

Symptoms We See in Fort Collins.

Pump Running Constantly.

Can indicate a failing check valve or a pump that's undersized for the actual water inflow.

Pump Not Turning On During a Storm.

A float switch failure is a common and often inexpensive fix.

No Backup Power Source.

A power outage during a major storm, exactly when you need the pump most, leaves a basement unprotected without battery backup.

Unusual Grinding or Rattling Noise.

Often a sign of impeller damage or debris in the pump housing.

Diagnosis.

How We Evaluate a Sump Pump System.

For an existing pump, a technician tests the float switch, check valve, and pump motor function directly rather than assuming based on age alone. For new installations, basin size and pump capacity get matched to the home's actual water table and drainage conditions, since an undersized pump in a high-risk neighborhood is a real liability during a genuine storm event.

Options.

Repair Existing Pump vs. New Installation With Battery Backup.

Repair Existing Pump.

  • Float switch, check valve, or motor repair
  • Lower cost when the pump itself is sound
  • Right call for a pump under 5-7 years old
  • Doesn't address a genuinely undersized system

New Installation With Battery Backup.

  • Sized correctly for your home's actual risk level
  • Battery backup protects against power outages during storms
  • Right call for homes in flood-prone neighborhoods
  • Higher upfront cost, meaningful protection during a real event

Testing Before You Actually Need It.

Testing Before You Actually Need It.

The worst time to discover a sump pump isn't working is during an active storm. Pouring a bucket of water into the basin every few months to confirm the float switch triggers the pump properly is a simple test that catches most failures well before they matter. For homes in Alta Vista, Buckingham, or other lower-lying areas, it's also worth confirming the battery backup actually holds a charge, since a backup battery that's quietly died sitting unused defeats the entire point of having one. We regularly see this paired with Frozen Pipe Repair on the same property.

FAQs.

Common Questions.

Do I really need a battery backup?

If your home is in one of Fort Collins' documented floodplain-adjacent neighborhoods, or if you've lost power during a storm before, a battery backup is a genuinely worthwhile addition.

How do I know if my current pump is undersized?

A technician can evaluate your basin, drainage inflow, and current pump capacity to confirm whether it's actually adequate.

Can you install a sump pump in a basement that doesn't have one?

Yes, new installations, including cutting a basin if needed, are a routine part of our work in the older, lower-lying neighborhoods.
