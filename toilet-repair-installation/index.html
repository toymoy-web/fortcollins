Common Toilet Problems We See

Running toilets are almost always a worn flapper or fill valve, an inexpensive fix that a lot of homeowners let run for months without realizing the water-bill impact. Recurring clogs, on the other hand, sometimes point to a partial blockage further down the line rather than the toilet itself, especially in older homes where the drain line hasn't been checked in years. This is worth reading alongside Faucet & Sink Repair, since the two issues share a lot of the same root causes.

Diagnosis

How We Diagnose a Toilet Problem

Most toilet repair calls are diagnosed visually within the tank itself, checking flapper seal, fill valve function, and water level. For recurring clogs, a technician checks whether the issue is isolated to the toilet or connected to a broader drain line problem before recommending anything more than a standard repair. Most visits resolve in well under an hour once the actual cause is identified, since toilet mechanisms are straightforward compared to more complex plumbing systems.

Component Repair

  • Low cost, usually under an hour
  • Right call for most tank-related issues
  • Doesn't help a cracked bowl or tank

Full Replacement

  • Chance to upgrade to a more efficient model
  • Higher upfront cost than a repair
  • Standard for toilets original to an older home

Spotting a Running Toilet Early

Spotting a Running Toilet Early

A running toilet is one of the easiest problems to catch early, since a quick check is all it takes. Adding a few drops of food coloring to the tank and checking the bowl a few minutes later without flushing will reveal a worn flapper immediately if color appears. Checking this every six months or so, especially on an older toilet, catches the problem well before it shows up as a noticeably higher water bill. The two problems are related closely enough that Plumbing Repair is worth a look too.

FAQs

Common Questions

How much water does a running toilet actually waste?

It varies, but a running toilet can waste a meaningful amount of water daily, which shows up as a real cost on your water bill over a billing cycle.

Can you install a toilet I've already purchased?

Yes, we can install a toilet you've purchased yourself or help you pick one during the visit.

Is a cracked toilet tank dangerous?

It can worsen over time and lead to a sudden leak, so replacement is the safer call once a crack is confirmed.

Can you match my existing toilet's bowl height and shape?

In most cases, yes, we carry a range of standard and comfort-height models and can special-order less common styles.
