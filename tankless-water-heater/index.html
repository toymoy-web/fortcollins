Local Cause.

Why Tankless Units Struggle Here.

Front Range water carries enough mineral content that tankless heat exchangers scale up faster here than in a soft-water region, and a unit that's never been descaled will eventually throw a flow or temperature error even though nothing is technically broken. Winter incoming-water temperatures also run colder here than in milder climates, which pushes some undersized units past their rated flow rate during a simultaneous shower-and-dishwasher scenario, a complaint we hear often in larger households. A fair number of these calls end up touching Water Heater Installation & Replacement too, so we look at both together.

Diagnosis.

How We Diagnose a Tankless Problem.

A technician reads the unit's fault history first, since most modern tankless heaters log recent error codes even after they clear. From there, flow rate and incoming water temperature get checked against the unit's rated capacity, because a lot of "broken" tankless heaters are actually just undersized for how the household uses hot water. If scale is suspected, a descale flush using the unit's service ports is the standard next step before any parts get replaced.

The Visit.

What to Expect During a Tankless Service Visit.

A service call starts with pulling the unit's fault history and asking what you've actually noticed, since "it stopped working" and "the water runs cold partway through a shower" point toward very different causes. For a descale, the technician connects to the unit's service ports, circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger, and flushes it clear, typically 45 minutes to an hour depending on how much scale has built up. For a suspected undersizing issue, we'll walk through your household's actual simultaneous hot water usage, how many showers or appliances run at once during a typical morning, and compare that honestly against your unit's rated capacity, rather than assuming a bigger, more expensive unit is automatically the fix.

Options.

Descale & Repair vs. Replacement or Upsizing.

Descale & Repair.

  • Lower cost when the unit itself is sound
  • Restores flow and temperature consistency
  • Recommended every 12-18 months on Front Range water
  • Doesn't fix a genuinely undersized unit

Replacement or Upsizing.

  • Fixes a unit that's outgrown your household's demand
  • Higher upfront cost than a descale
  • Newer units run more efficiently
  • Right call when the unit is past its rated lifespan

Keeping a Tankless Unit Running Longer.

Keeping a Tankless Unit Running Longer.

The single biggest thing a Fort Collins homeowner can do for a tankless unit is stick to a descale schedule rather than waiting for an error code to force the issue. Front Range mineral content means scale accumulates on a predictable timeline, and a flush every 12 to 18 months is far cheaper than letting the heat exchanger scale up to the point of failure. It's also worth having your actual peak hot water demand checked against the unit's rated flow, since a lot of "failing" tankless units are simply being asked to do more than they were sized for, especially in a household that's grown since the unit was installed. This is worth reading alongside Water Heater Repair, since the two issues share a lot of the same root causes.

FAQs.

Common Questions.

How often should a tankless unit be descaled here?

Every 12 to 18 months on Front Range water is the general rule, sooner if you notice reduced flow or an error code.

Can you repair any brand?

We service Rheem, A.O. Smith, Rinnai, and Navien tankless units, among others, and stock common parts for the models we see most.

Is tankless worth switching to from a tank?

It depends on your household's peak hot water demand and how long you plan to stay in the home. We'll give you a straight answer on the phone before quoting.
