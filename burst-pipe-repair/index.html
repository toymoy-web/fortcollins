Local Cause

Why Pipes Burst in Fort Collins Winters

Front Range winters put real freeze-thaw stress on exposed or poorly insulated pipe runs, especially in older homes in floodplain-adjacent neighborhoods like Alta Vista and Buckingham where original plumbing was never designed with today's insulation standards in mind. A pipe doesn't need a hard freeze for days to burst. A single cold snap hitting an exposed line in a crawlspace, garage, or exterior wall is often enough. We regularly see this paired with Frozen Pipe Repair on the same property.

Diagnosis

What Happens on an Emergency Burst-Pipe Call

The first priority on any burst pipe call is locating and closing the correct shutoff valve to stop active water damage, which sometimes means the main shutoff rather than a fixture-specific valve depending on where the break is. Once water is stopped, the exact break point gets identified and repaired, and if the burst was caused by freezing, we'll flag any other exposed lines at similar risk before we leave.

Winterizing Exposed Pipe Runs

Winterizing Exposed Pipe Runs

The most effective prevention against a burst pipe is addressing exposure before winter hits, not after a pipe has already split. Foam pipe insulation on exposed runs in a crawlspace, garage, or exterior wall costs very little compared to the cleanup after a burst, and it's a straightforward add-on during any other plumbing visit. Letting a faucet drip slightly during an especially hard cold snap can also relieve pressure in a marginal line, buying time until warmer weather returns. It's common enough to also need Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement that we always ask about it during diagnosis.
