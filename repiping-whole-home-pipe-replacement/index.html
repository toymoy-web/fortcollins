Local Cause

When Repiping Becomes the Right Call

Homes in Fort Collins' older neighborhoods, particularly those built before the 1970s, often still run original galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside over decades and eventually restrict flow or spring pinhole leaks in multiple spots. Once a home has had more than one or two unrelated pipe leaks in recent years, repiping usually becomes more cost-effective than continuing to chase individual repairs.

Signs to Watch For

Symptoms We See in Fort Collins

Recurring Pinhole Leaks in Different Spots

A sign the pipe material itself is failing, not just one bad section.

Rusty Water From Multiple Fixtures

Common with aging galvanized pipe corroding from the inside.

Noticeably Reduced Water Pressure Over Time

Corrosion narrowing the interior diameter of galvanized pipe.

Visible Galvanized Pipe in the Basement or Crawlspace

Worth an evaluation even before any leaks appear.

Diagnosis

How We Evaluate a Home for Repiping

A technician inspects accessible pipe sections, checks the home's age and known leak history, and tests water pressure and flow at multiple fixtures. If galvanized pipe is confirmed and showing the expected signs of age-related corrosion, we'll walk through whether a full repipe or a phased approach, tackling the worst sections first, makes more sense for your situation and budget.

Options

Phased Repiping vs. Full Whole-Home Repipe

Phased Repiping

  • Tackles the worst sections first
  • Spreads cost over time
  • Right call when budget is a real constraint
  • Some risk of continued leaks in untouched sections

Full Whole-Home Repipe

  • Addresses the entire system at once
  • Higher upfront cost but no remaining old pipe risk
  • Typically uses PEX or copper
  • Recommended once multiple leaks have occurred

Deciding When to Stop Waiting

Deciding When to Stop Waiting

There's no perfect moment to repipe a home, but a pattern is worth watching for: if you've had two unrelated pipe leaks within a couple of years, that's usually a sign the underlying pipe material, not bad luck, is the real problem. Waiting for a third leak to decide rarely saves money once you account for repeated repair visits and potential water damage from a leak that goes unnoticed for a while. We'll always give you a straight answer about whether your specific situation has reached that point.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if I actually have galvanized pipe?

A technician can identify it during an inspection, or you can check an exposed section in the basement, it typically looks dull gray and may show rust streaking.

Does repiping mean tearing out my walls?

Some wall access is usually needed, but a lot of the work happens through existing openings and crawlspaces where possible, minimizing repair after the fact.

Can this be done in phases instead of all at once?

Yes, phased repiping is a reasonable option when a full repipe isn't in the budget right now.
