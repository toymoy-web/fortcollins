Local Cause.

When Repiping Becomes the Right Call.

Homes in Fort Collins' older neighborhoods, particularly those built before the 1970s, often still run original galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside over decades and eventually restrict flow or spring pinhole leaks in multiple spots. Once a home has had more than one or two unrelated pipe leaks in recent years, repiping usually becomes more cost-effective than continuing to chase individual repairs. Any repipe work is performed to the Colorado Plumbing Code, the state's adopted version of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets the material and installation standards a repipe has to meet. This overlaps with Water Line Repair & Replacement often enough that we check for it on the same visit.

Diagnosis.

How We Evaluate a Home for Repiping.

A technician inspects accessible pipe sections, checks the home's age and known leak history, and tests water pressure and flow at multiple fixtures. If galvanized pipe is confirmed and showing the expected signs of age-related corrosion, we'll walk through whether a full repipe or a phased approach, tackling the worst sections first, makes more sense for your situation and budget.

Options.

Phased Repiping vs. Full Whole-Home Repipe.

Phased Repiping.

  • Tackles the worst sections first
  • Spreads cost over time
  • Right call when budget is a real constraint
  • Some risk of continued leaks in untouched sections

Full Whole-Home Repipe.

  • Addresses the entire system at once
  • Higher upfront cost but no remaining old pipe risk
  • Typically uses PEX or copper
  • Recommended once multiple leaks have occurred

Deciding When to Stop Waiting.

Deciding When to Stop Waiting.

There's no perfect moment to repipe a home, but a pattern is worth watching for: if you've had two unrelated pipe leaks within a couple of years, that's usually a sign the underlying pipe material, not bad luck, is the real problem. Waiting for a third leak to decide rarely saves money once you account for repeated repair visits and potential water damage from a leak that goes unnoticed for a while. We'll always give you a straight answer about whether your specific situation has reached that point. We regularly see this paired with Burst Pipe Repair on the same property.

What to Do Before We Arrive for a Leak Emergency.

  • Shut off the main water valve if a leak is actively spreading, especially near outlets or a breaker panel.
  • Never ignore water pooling near electrical wiring. Water and electricity together are a real shock and fire risk.
  • If you smell a musty odor behind a wall with no visible leak, treat it as a possible hidden leak and call rather than wait.
  • Keep a record of every leak's date and location. A pattern of two or more in different spots is worth mentioning on the call.
FAQs.

Common Questions.

How do I know if I actually have galvanized pipe?

A technician can identify it during an inspection, or you can check an exposed section in the basement, it typically looks dull gray and may show rust streaking.

Does repiping mean tearing out my walls?

Some wall access is usually needed, but a lot of the work happens through existing openings and crawlspaces where possible, minimizing repair after the fact.

Can this be done in phases instead of all at once?

Yes, phased repiping is a reasonable option when a full repipe isn't in the budget right now.
