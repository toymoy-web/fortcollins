Local Risk Data

Fort Collins Flash-Flood & Storm Risk

Current Season Level: MODERATE

Fort Collins' highest flash-flood water-damage risk runs June through August, the same window that produced the Spring Creek Flood of July 28, 1997, when 10 to 14 inches of rain fell in roughly 31 hours, according to National Weather Service storm records and Colorado State University's own historical account of the event. That rainfall total still stands as Colorado's record for a single urban-area storm. Five people died, the city absorbed close to $200 million in total damage, and Colorado State University's library alone lost about $140 million in books and equipment. Alta Vista and Buckingham, two of Fort Collins' older neighborhoods, remain inside the official 100-year floodplain today according to the city's own 2005 Northside Neighborhoods Plan.

If your basement has taken on water during a summer storm, that history is usually the reason, not bad luck. Ask about your specific address's flood-zone status when you call.

Pricing

What Actually Drives Your Price

A quote over the phone is a rough range at best. The real number depends on a handful of factors a technician confirms on site:

  • The age and material of your existing pipes. Older galvanized lines generally cost more to repair than modern PEX or copper.
  • How the problem gets diagnosed. A camera inspection or leak-detection equipment call adds real diagnostic time to the visit.
  • Parts availability for your specific water heater or fixture brand.
  • Whether the call happens during standard hours or after-hours emergency dispatch.
  • Permit requirements on larger jobs like repiping or gas line work.

On insurance: most homeowner policies cover sudden, accidental damage like a burst pipe, but not gradual wear, and not always the plumbing repair itself. Ask your adjuster what your specific policy actually covers before assuming either way.
