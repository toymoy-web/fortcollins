Local Cause

Why Disposals Jam or Leak

Jams are almost always caused by something fibrous or hard getting caught in the flywheel, from fruit pits to fibrous vegetable scraps that a disposal isn't built to handle. Leaks, on the other hand, usually come from a worn seal at the sink flange or the connection to the dishwasher line, both of which degrade with age rather than failing suddenly. Either issue is usually diagnosable within a few minutes once a technician actually has eyes on the unit. It's common enough to also need Drain Cleaning & Unclogging that we always ask about it during diagnosis.

Signs to Watch For

Symptoms We See in Fort Collins

Humming But Not Spinning

Usually a jam in the flywheel, often fixable by manually clearing the obstruction.

Leaking From the Bottom

Typically means the unit's internal seal has failed and replacement is the practical fix.

Leaking at the Sink Flange

A worn seal at the connection point, sometimes repairable without replacing the whole unit.

Unusual Grinding Noise

Can indicate something metal or hard lodged in the disposal, worth checking before running it again.

Diagnosis

How We Diagnose a Disposal Problem

A technician checks whether the unit is jammed, leaking, or has failed electrically, since each has a different fix. A jam is often clearable on the spot. A leak from the housing itself usually means the unit has reached the end of its service life, since disposal seals aren't practically field-repairable once they fail internally. We'll always attempt the simpler, lower-cost fix first and only recommend replacement once that's genuinely ruled out.

Options

Jam Clearing vs. Full Replacement

Jam Clearing

  • Fast, low-cost fix for a stuck flywheel
  • No replacement parts usually needed
  • Right call when the unit hums but won't spin
  • Doesn't address an internal leak

Full Replacement

  • Standard fix for an internal seal leak
  • Chance to upgrade horsepower or noise level
  • Includes disconnect and reconnect of plumbing
  • Recommended for units near end of life

Keeping a Disposal Running Longer

Keeping a Disposal Running Longer

Running cold water for a few seconds before and after using a disposal helps flush debris fully through the line rather than letting it settle. Fibrous scraps like celery, corn husks, and onion skins are worth putting in the trash instead, since they're the most common cause of jams we see. Running ice cubes through occasionally helps knock loose buildup on the blades without needing a service call. A disposal that's cared for this way typically outlasts one that isn't by several years. FAQs

Common Questions

Can a jammed disposal usually be fixed without replacement?

Yes, most jams clear without needing a new unit, using the reset button and manual flywheel clearing.

What should never go down a garbage disposal?

Fibrous vegetable scraps, fruit pits, and grease are the most common causes of jams and clogs we see.

How long does a replacement take?

Most disposal replacements take under an hour, including reconnecting the dishwasher line if present.

Can you upgrade to a more powerful disposal?

Yes, upgrading horsepower during a replacement is common and can reduce future jamming on tougher food waste.
