What Makes Basement Plumbing Different Here

Homes in Fort Collins' older, lower-lying neighborhoods often have basement plumbing that predates current drainage standards, meaning floor drains and ejector pump systems installed decades ago weren't necessarily sized for how the neighborhood's drainage has changed over time. A basement bathroom addition in these areas also typically requires an ejector pump system, since gravity drainage usually isn't an option below the main sewer line's depth. A fair number of these calls end up touching Sump Pump Installation & Repair too, so we look at both together.

Diagnosis

How We Approach a Basement Plumbing Project

For new basement bathroom rough-ins, we confirm the sewer line's depth relative to the basement floor first, since that determines whether gravity drainage is possible or an ejector pump system is required. For existing floor drain or ejector pump issues, a technician tests the pump's function and checks for blockage or capacity issues in the connected drainage line. Getting that depth confirmation right at the start avoids the far more expensive mistake of discovering a gravity-drain assumption was wrong partway through a remodel.

Keeping Basement Drainage Working

Keeping Basement Drainage Working

A basement floor drain that's rarely used can dry out at the trap, letting sewer gas smell come up through it even without an actual backup. Pouring a cup of water down an infrequently used floor drain every couple of months keeps the trap sealed and avoids a false alarm that looks like a bigger problem than it is. For homes with an ejector pump, an annual check of the pump and float switch catches most failures before a heavy rain puts real demand on the system. This is worth reading alongside Water Damage Restoration, since the two issues share a lot of the same root causes.

What to Do Before We Arrive for a Backup

  • Avoid direct contact with backed-up water. Sewage-adjacent water can carry real health risks.
  • Turn off electrical circuits serving the affected area if you can do so without stepping in water.
  • Don't run washing machines, showers, or other fixtures that drain to the same line until it's cleared.
  • Ventilate the area if possible while you wait, opening a window or running a fan positioned outside the affected room.
FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need an ejector pump for a basement bathroom?

In most Fort Collins basement layouts, yes, since the sewer line typically sits above the basement floor level, making gravity drainage impossible.

Why does my floor drain back up during heavy rain?

It can be a capacity issue, a blockage, or a drainage change in the surrounding area over time. We'll diagnose the actual cause during a visit.

Can you coordinate with my basement finishing contractor?

Yes, we regularly handle the plumbing rough-in portion of basement finishing projects alongside a general contractor's timeline.
