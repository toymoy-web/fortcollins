Local Cause

Why Water Heaters Fail Here

Most tank water heaters in this area fail from ordinary sediment buildup and age rather than any one dramatic cause, though homes in the older floodplain-adjacent neighborhoods like Alta Vista and Buckingham sometimes carry original units well past a safe service life simply because nobody's replaced them yet. A unit past 10 to 12 years old is a reasonable candidate for replacement even before it fails outright, especially if it's already showing rust at the base or the pilot struggles to stay lit.

Signs to Watch For

Symptoms We See in Fort Collins

No Hot Water at All

Often a failed heating element, thermocouple, or a tripped high-limit switch, sometimes fixable, sometimes not worth repairing on an old unit.

Rusty or Discolored Water

Usually internal tank corrosion, a sign the tank itself is failing rather than a single part.

Popping or Rumbling Noises

Sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank, common on units that have never been flushed.

Water Pooling at the Base

A leaking tank almost always means replacement, not repair, since the tank itself has failed.

Diagnosis

How We Decide Repair Versus Replace

A technician checks the unit's age, the extent of any rust or leaking, and whether the failure is a single replaceable part like a thermocouple or element versus the tank itself. A unit under 6 years old with a clear single-part failure is usually worth repairing. A unit over 10 years old that's leaking, rusting, or has failed more than once in the last year is usually not, and we'll tell you that honestly instead of billing you for a repair that won't hold.

The Visit

What to Expect on Installation Day

Most installations start with confirming the new unit's specifications against your home's existing venting, gas or electrical connections, and available space, since a mismatch there is the most common reason an install takes longer than expected. The old unit gets drained and disconnected first, then removed and set aside for haul-away. The new unit is set, connected, and tested under full operating pressure before the technician leaves, including checking for any leaks at every fitting. For a switch from tank to tankless, expect a longer visit, since venting and sometimes gas line sizing typically need to be adjusted to match the new unit's requirements.

Getting the Most Life Out of a New Unit

Getting the Most Life Out of a New Unit

A new water heater's lifespan depends heavily on maintenance most homeowners never do. Flushing a tank unit annually to clear sediment before it hardens at the bottom is the single most effective thing you can do, and it takes a technician under an hour during a routine visit. If you're in one of Fort Collins' harder-water pockets, pairing a new tank with a whole-home softener can meaningfully extend it past the standard 10 to 12 year lifespan. We'll flag your specific water hardness during installation and let you know honestly whether that upgrade is worth it for your situation.
