Emergency Water Damage Restoration in Fort Collins, CO

Fort Collins has a documented reason to take water damage seriously that most cities don't: the Spring Creek Flood of July 28, 1997, when 10 to 14 inches of rain fell in roughly 31 hours, according to National Weather Service storm records and Colorado State University's own historical account, a total that still stands as Colorado's record for a single urban storm. Five people died, the city absorbed close to $200 million in damage, and Colorado State University's own library lost about $140 million in books and equipment. Local Cause

Why Certain Fort Collins Neighborhoods Flood

Alta Vista and Buckingham, two of the city's older neighborhoods, remain inside the official 100-year floodplain today according to the city's own 2005 Northside Neighborhoods Plan, a designation with real roots in how Fort Collins grew up around the Cache la Poudre River. Working-class neighborhoods were historically built low near the water while wealthier postwar development, like Sheely, was deliberately built up on higher ground specifically to avoid flood risk. If your home sits in one of the older, lower-lying neighborhoods, that history is directly relevant to your actual flood exposure, not just a historical footnote. We regularly see this paired with Water Mitigation on the same property.

Diagnosis

How Water Mitigation Actually Works

The first step is always extraction, using pumps and wet vacuums to remove standing water before anything else. From there, moisture meters check how far water has traveled into drywall, subfloor, and framing, since visible wet spots almost always understate the real spread. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers then run until moisture readings return to a safe baseline, not just until the floor looks dry.

The Visit

What Happens in the First Hour

The first hour of a water damage call is almost entirely about stopping active damage, not starting repairs. If the source is still running, whether that's a burst pipe or water still entering from outside, shutting it off comes before anything else. Standing water then gets extracted with pumps and wet vacuums while a moisture meter maps how far the water has actually traveled into walls, subfloor, and framing, since visible wet areas consistently understate the real spread. Only once extraction is underway does the conversation turn to drying equipment placement and, if needed, coordinating with your insurance adjuster on documentation.

Lowering Your Flood Risk Before the Next Storm

Lowering Your Flood Risk Before the Next Storm

Because Fort Collins' worst flood risk is seasonal and well documented, there's real value in addressing vulnerability before June rolls around rather than after a basement floods. A working sump pump with battery backup is the single most effective safeguard for homes in Alta Vista, Buckingham, or any other lower-lying area, since a power outage during a major storm is exactly when a standard pump fails you. Grading and downspout extensions that carry water away from the foundation are a low-cost second layer of protection worth checking every spring. If your home has flooded before, we can also flag specific entry points worth addressing before the next monsoon season. It's common enough to also need Frozen Pipe Repair that we always ask about it during diagnosis.

If Water Is Actively Entering Your Home

  • Shut off electricity to the affected area at the breaker if you can reach it without standing in water.
  • Avoid walking through standing water near outlets, cords, or a wet electrical panel.
  • Move furniture, electronics, and valuables to dry ground if it's safe to do so quickly.
  • Photograph the damage before you start any cleanup, for your insurance claim.
  • Don't use a household vacuum on standing water. It's not built for it and creates a shock risk.
