Local Cause

What Actually Causes Most Faucet Leaks

Most dripping faucets trace back to a worn cartridge, O-ring, or valve seat, parts that wear out from ordinary use over years rather than any sudden failure. Front Range mineral content in the water can also accelerate wear on internal faucet components compared to a soft-water area, which is part of why faucets here sometimes need this kind of repair sooner than the manufacturer's expected lifespan. Signs to Watch For

Symptoms We See in Fort Collins

Continuous Drip From the Spout

Usually a worn cartridge or washer inside the faucet body.

Leak at the Base of the Faucet

Often a worn O-ring rather than the main valve mechanism.

Low Pressure at One Faucet Only

Frequently a clogged aerator, an easy fix, rather than a whole-house issue.

Handle That's Loose or Hard to Turn

Can indicate a worn cartridge or a valve stem that needs replacement.

Diagnosis

How We Diagnose a Faucet or Sink Leak

A technician isolates whether the leak is coming from the spout, the base, or the supply connection underneath, since each points to a different worn part. Aerators get checked first for pressure complaints, since that's the simplest and cheapest cause to rule out before assuming a larger issue. Most faucet diagnostics take only a few minutes once the fixture is opened up, since the range of likely causes is fairly narrow compared to whole-system plumbing issues.

Extending a Faucet's Working Life

Extending a Faucet's Working Life

A faucet's internal cartridge wears out faster with harder water, so a home without a softener will generally see faucet issues sooner than one with treated water. Cleaning an aerator every few months prevents the mineral buildup that often gets mistaken for a bigger pressure problem. If you notice a faucet handle getting harder to turn well before it starts leaking, that's usually an early sign the cartridge is wearing out, worth addressing before it fails completely and starts dripping.
